# The form the material already has

Theology has its own study loop, and it is older and better than anything a study-
skills literature would supply. This is what it is, how the faculties examine it, and
how a source's weight is judged. Where something could not be verified it says so
rather than filling it in.

---

## 1. The *articulus* as a study loop

The unit of the *Summa* is not the *quaestio* but the **articulus**. A *quaestio*
gathers related problems under a theme; each *articulus* poses one issue narrow enough
to answer yes or no.

| Move | Opens | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Title | *Utrum…* | States the disputable point |
| Objections | *Videtur quod…* | Arguments for the position Thomas will **not** hold |
| *Sed contra* | *Sed contra est quod…* | A short counter-citation — Scripture, a Father, a council, Aristotle |
| *Respondeo* | *Respondeo dicendum quod…* | The *determinatio*: his own resolution, usually by drawing a distinction |
| Replies | *Ad primum ergo dicendum…* | Each objection answered in turn |

**Why to start from the objections.** The form is the transcript of a live classroom
practice: the *quaestio disputata* was the principal means of teaching in the medieval
university, an oral contest in which an *opponens* pressed objections and a *respondens*
replied, the master closing with a *determinatio* on a later day. The objections
therefore carry the pedagogical weight — **they are the reason the question is a
question at all.** Until the student has felt the force of the *videtur quod*, the
*respondeo* answers nothing. Note also that they are ordered by *kind* of authority —
typically a scriptural or patristic text, then reason, then a common-sense difficulty
— so the reader is being shown which sources generate the tension.

**The *sed contra* is authority, not proof.** This is the move most often misread. It
establishes that the contrary position is not eccentric, and so licenses the inquiry;
it shifts the burden, it does not discharge it. Thomas does not always fully agree with
it and does not consistently reply to it. **The proof is the *respondeo*.**

**The *respondeo* reframes.** It usually does not pick a side of the *utrum* as posed;
it defines the key term, distinguishes a sense in which the answer is yes from a sense
in which it is no, and then resolves. Ask: how does he reformulate or qualify the
question, and what distinction does he introduce?

**The replies are the real work.** The *respondeo* establishes the conclusion; the
*ad primum…ad secundum* establish that the conclusion costs nothing the student was
right to hold. Each reply locates the precise equivocation, false premise or misapplied
authority — without denying whatever truth the objection carried. **An answer that
cannot dispose of each objection individually has not been reached.** Pieper puts the
ethic of it as "listening to the interlocutor; respecting his argument and person;
addressing oneself to him; refraining from arbitrary jargon; seeking clarity, not
sensationalism."

[Hochschild's reading questions](https://www.joshhochschild.com/summatips) ·

### Navigating it

Three Parts, the Second divided into *Prima Secundae* and *Secunda Secundae*, the
unfinished Third completed by a *Supplementum* compiled from the *Scriptum on the
Sentences* — some 614 questions and 3,125 articles with the Supplement. The macro-shape
is **I** God and creation (the *exitus*); **I-II and II-II** the rational creature's
movement to God, general then particular; **III** Christ, the sacraments, the last
things (the *reditus*).

The **ordo disciplinae** is Thomas's own stated principle, from the Prologue: he writes
for beginners, and complains that what they need "is not taught *according to the
order of the subject-matter*, but according as the plan of the book might require."
So **order is doctrine**: where a question sits tells you what he takes to be already
settled. Reading II-II on a particular virtue without I-II on habit, virtue and law is
reading the conclusion of an argument the student has not followed. Anchor each article
twice — against its question, and against its treatise.

### Citing it

Part → question → article → part of the article:

- `ST I-II, q. 94, a. 2, co.` — the body (*corpus*); `resp.` means the same
- `ST I-II, q. 94, a. 2, ad 1` — the reply to the first objection
- `ST I, q. 1, a. 8, arg. 2` (or `obj. 2`) — the second objection
- `ST II-II, q. 4, a. 1, s.c.` — the *sed contra*
- `aa. 1-5` for a range; some houses write `Ia`, `IaIIae`, `IIaIIae`, `IIIa`

Two conventions worth keeping: **only the *Summa* may be cited by internal reference
alone** — other works of Aquinas need the internal reference *and* an edition and page;
and there is no single mandated format for medieval texts, so pick one and be
consistent rather than claiming a universal standard. The library's own house form
governs where it differs: see `04. Reference/citation-guide.md`, and resolve the
anchor through the `search` skill.
[St Thomas guide](https://libguides.stthomas.edu/c.php?g=88920&p=573375) ·
[ThomisticMetaphysics](https://www.thomisticmetaphysics.com/how-to-cite/citing-aquinas)

## 2. How the faculties actually examine

**Credits.** ECTS counts *total* workload, not contact hours. Santa Croce states it
plainly: "Ogni credito ECTS corrisponde a **25 ore di studio**, comprese le lezioni e
le esercitazioni scritte" — each credit is 25 hours of study, lectures and written
exercises included. So a 3-credit course meeting for ~24 hours implies roughly **50
hours of private work**, about two hours per contact hour; a semester capped at 30 ECTS
is 750 hours. Angelicum: S.T.B. minimum 180 ECTS in theology, of which 12 for the
comprehensive exam; S.T.L. 121 ECTS including the *tesina* and the *lectio coram*.
*Veritatis Gaudium* itself does not define ECTS or mandate the comprehensive exam —
art. 43 leaves it to each faculty's plan of studies; it was *Sapientia Christiana*'s
*Normae* art. 53 that required "un examen global de todas las disciplinas o una prueba
equivalente" at the end of each cycle.
[PUSC ISSRA](https://www.pusc.it/issra/corso-scienze-religiose/piano-degli-studi-triennio) ·
[Angelicum STB](https://angelicum.it/teologia/stb/) · [STL](https://angelicum.it/teologia/stl/) ·
[Veritatis Gaudium](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/apost_constitutions/documents/papa-francesco_costituzione-ap_20171208_veritatis-gaudium.html)

**Temario / tesario.** A fixed, published, numbered list of themes constituting the
examinable syllabus of the **degree** exam — not of any one course. It cuts across
dogmatics, Scripture, moral, history, patristics and canon law. Its items are often
called *tesis*: Burgos's "consta de **50 tesis**". A theme is a **title plus
sub-points** — the skeleton of a 20–30 minute lecture. Santa Croce's tema 1:

> **1. Natura della teologia.** L'oggetto e le fonti della teologia. Il ruolo della fede
> nella razionalità teologica… La teologia come scienza in Tommaso d'Aquino… Rapporti
> fra teologia e Magistero: i contenuti dell'Istruzione della CDF *Donum Veritatis* (1990)

Navarra's tema 1 is the same shape: "Naturaleza de la teología… **Método propio de la
Teología: las fuentes teológicas**." Note what both faculties do: they make **the
weighting of sources the first examinable item**. Note too that PUSC's themes name the
primary texts inside the theme, so the temario is already a reading list — but
**neither temario carries a bibliography**; that lives in the course syllabi and the
manual.
[PUSC, 50 temi](https://www.pusc.it/sites/default/files/teo/Temi_Baccalaureato.pdf) ·
[Navarra temario](https://www.unav.edu/documents/29050/1093110/TEMARIO+PRUEBA+DE+GRADO+DE+BACHILLER+TEO.pdf)

**The oral exam.** Santa Croce, verbatim: the exam is oral, in public session, before
two or three professors; "ogni studente potrà **scegliere un tema fra i due estratti a
sorte** dall'elenco dei **50 temi**"; he has **20 minutes to prepare**, during which he
may draft an outline "con l'ausilio delle **fonti in formato cartaceo (la Bibbia e i
documenti del Magistero)**", no electronic devices; then **20 minutes** to expound it
and answer the commission's questions. The final Baccalaureate mark is 17/18 the
weighted course average and 1/18 this.
[PUSC, Norme prova di grado](https://www.pusc.it/sites/default/files/teo/Norme%20prova%20Bacc%20%28stud%29.pdf)

| | Draw | Prep | Exposition | Interrogation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PUSC** (STB) | 2 of 50 by lot, student picks 1 | 20 min, Bible + magisterium on paper | 20 min including questions | with the exposition |
| **Navarra** (STL) | *sorteo* on the day of the *tesina* defence | — | 30 min | on that theme "y otros"; *apto / no apto* |
| **Valladolid / Burgos** (STB) | written: 1 of 3 at random, 3 hrs, Bible + Denzinger-Hünermann + Vatican II permitted | — | — | oral ~1 hr, 4 professors × ~15 min |
| **Angelicum** (STL, *lectio coram*) | student submits 20 topics, dean picks 3, student picks 1 | — | 40 min *lectio* | 10 min on the *lectio* + 20 min on the *tesario* |

Two things the faculties provide, and both are worth imitating: a **dedicated synthesis
seminar** in the final year, and an **explicit start date** for tesario work — Angelicum
tells students to begin in the first semester of STL 2.

**The loop this yields**, and it maps exactly onto the *articulus*: draw a theme → 20
minutes to build an outline from primary sources only → expound → be cross-examined.
The theme is the *utrum*, the cross-examination supplies the objections, the exposition
is the *respondeo*, and the replies under questioning are the *ad primum*. At every
institution in the table, **the replies are what decides the grade.**

**The manual.** The faculty's designated systematic text. PUSC is explicit: "Lo studente
deve **studiare principalmente i testi-base**, in quanto lo studio ordinato di questi
testi è indispensabile per un apprendimento metodico e sicuro." Each manual carries a
*guida didattica* saying what is essential; each subject requires written exercises in
proportion to its credits; and "gli **esami orali** rappresentano **l'ultimo atto del
processo di apprendimento**." The bibliography is explicitly not the manual and not
exhaustive: "I testi indicati in bibliografia sono **esclusivamente quelli di base**."
So the layering is **primary sources ← manual (which orders and weights them) ←
bibliografía ← the teacher's own emphasis**, and the manual's specific function is to
tell the student which primary text is the *locus classicus* for a given thesis.
[PUSC, Metodologia per lo studio](https://www.pusc.it/issra/corso-scienze-religiose/metodologia-per-lo-studio)

**Theses in the manual tradition.** In Tanquerey and Ott the unit of exposition is a
numbered thesis developed in a fixed order: *status quaestionis* → *adversarii* → the
*nota theologica* → proof from Scripture, from tradition, from the magisterium, from
theological reason → objections answered. **This sequence is a reconstruction from the
manuals' own tables of contents, not a quotation from a source that states it** — treat
it as substantially reliable and the wording as unverified. Structurally it is the
*articulus* institutionalised: a proposition, the opposition named, the sources
marshalled in order of authority, the objections answered last.

## 3. Weighing a source: *loci theologici* and the *notae*

Melchor Cano's *De locis theologicis* (Salamanca, 1563) enumerates **ten loci**:

**Proper** — 1. Scripture · 2. apostolic traditions · 3. the Catholic Church ·
4. councils · 5. the Roman Church and papal magisterium · 6. the Fathers ·
7. scholastic theologians and canonists.
**Annexed** — 8. natural reason · 9. philosophers and jurists · 10. human history.

*(The old* Catholic Encyclopedia *gives a variant list, folding natural reason into the
seven proper loci. Gardeil and the current references agree against it; follow them,
and note the discrepancy rather than hiding it.)*

The operative part is the **gradation by probative force**, from Gardeil:

| | Loci | Force |
|---|---|---|
| **Constitutive** | Scripture, apostolic tradition | They *contain* revelation |
| **Declarative, certain** | the Church, councils, the papacy | They *declare* infallibly what revelation contains |
| **Declarative, probable** | Fathers, scholastics | Probable principles. Highest patristic certainty belongs to **unanimity** |
| **Annexed** | reason, philosophers, history | "Solicited from another domain" |

Cano's own caution is worth carrying: "a firm locus for arguments sometimes emerges as
not firm."

Downstream of this is the **nota theologica** — the grade of certainty a proposition
carries, and the censure attaching to its denial: *de fide divina et catholica definita*
(denial: heresy) · *de fide divina et catholica* · *fidei proxima* · *de fide
ecclesiastica* · *doctrina catholica* · *theologice certa* · *sententia communis* ·
*sententia probabilis*. **Never state more certainty than the source carries, and never
let the student's wording claim more than his source does.** The vocabulary has shifted
toward *Lumen gentium* 25 and *Donum veritatis* (1990), but the discipline is the same —
and, as above, both Navarra and PUSC make it the *first* thing a theology graduate must
be able to expound.
[Loci theologici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loci_theologici) ·
[Gardeil, tr.](https://www.athomist.com/articles/the-de-locis-theologicis-its-nature-history-aftermath-and-potential-future) ·
[Theological notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theological_notes)

## 4. What could not be verified

State these as gaps rather than filling them, and check before relying on one:

- Torrell's own wording on the *ordo disciplinae* — the position is reported reliably
  at second hand; no verbatim quotation was obtained.
- The Latin of the *Summa*'s Prologue against a critical edition. *Secundum ordinem
  disciplinae* is the standard reading but was checked only in English translation.
- Freddoso's "Structure of Quaestio" handout (403) and the Thomistic Institute's
  *Aquinas 101* lesson on reading an article (now behind a sign-in): both exist, neither
  could be read.
- Specific reading advice from McInerny and page-level Pieper.
- The Gregoriana's ECTS figure — asserted in secondary sources, not confirmed on a
  readable page. The PUSC figure quoted above is from its ISSRA (lower-cycle) pages.
- The Angelicum's actual *tesario* PDFs — every URL found returned 404; they may be
  behind the student portal. The Handbook of Studies is the place to look.
- The canonical manual-thesis template (§2, above).
- The discrepancy in Cano's ten loci (§3, above).
