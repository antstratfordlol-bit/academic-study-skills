# Theological & Liturgical Translation Glossary

This glossary establishes canonical English renderings for academic theological texts translated from Italian, Spanish, and Latin, specifically tailored to the dogmatic and liturgical-sacramental traditions of the Pontifical University of the Holy Cross (PUSC, Prof. Juan Rego) and the magisterial patrimony of the Church.

## 3. Latin Dogmatic & Liturgical Formulas (Retain in Italics)

All Latin formulas and locus phrases must remain in their original Latin text and be consistently italicised. Never translate or anglicise these technical formulas:

- *actuosa participatio*: Full, conscious, and active participation in liturgical celebrations (*Sacrosanctum Concilium*, n. 14).
- *ex opere operato*: By the very fact of the action being performed; objective sacramental efficacy.
- *ex opere operantis*: According to the disposition of the agent receiving or conferring the sacrament.
- *in persona Christi capitis*: In the person of Christ the Head; describing the sacerdotal ministry.
- *res et sacramentum*: The intermediate sacramental reality (e.g. the Eucharistic presence, the sacramental character).
- *sacramentum tantum*: The visible sign only (bread and wine, water, matter and form).
- *res tantum*: The ultimate grace or reality conferred (grace, charity, communion).
- *mysterium fidei*: The mystery of faith.
- *lex orandi, lex credendi*: The law of prayer is the law of belief.
- *lex credendi, lex vivendi*: The law of belief is the law of life.
- *nexus mysteriorum*: The interconnected harmony of Christian mysteries.
- *imago Dei*: Image of God.
- *gratia elevans*: Elevating grace, raising nature to supernatural life.
- *gratia sanans*: Healing grace, restoring wounded human nature.
- *capax Dei*: Capable of God, endowed with an innate capacity for divine communion.
- *status naturae integrae*: The state of integral nature (before the Fall).
- *status naturae lapsae*: The state of fallen nature.
- *status naturae redemptae*: The state of redeemed nature.
- *transubstantiatio*: Transubstantiation, the unique substantial conversion of bread and wine.
- *communicatio idiomatum*: The communication of idioms / properties between the two natures in Christ's divine Person.
- *anakephalaiosis*: Recapitulation of all things in Christ (St Irenaeus).
- *analogia fidei*: The analogy of faith.
- *analogia entis*: The analogy of being.
- *sacrificium laudis*: Sacrifice of praise.
- *sub speciebus*: Under the sacramental species.

## 4. Stylistic & Syntactic Rules

1. **British English Orthography**:
   Always apply British English spelling conventions across all translated texts:
   - *neighbour*, *colour*, *honour*, *favour*, *splendour*
   - *realise*, *organise*, *harmonise*, *characterise*
   - *centre*, *theatre*, *metre*
   - *defence*, *offence*
   - *programme* (except computer programming)
   - *travelling*, *counselled*

2. **Absolute Ban on Em Dashes**:
   Never use em dashes (`—`) in translation prose. Render complex parenthetical clauses using:
   - Colons for explanations or consequences
   - Semicolons for balanced, coordinated propositions
   - Brackets or parentheses for brief qualifications
   - Natural commas or full stops for clarity

3. **No Condensation or Truncation**:
   Scholarly theological arguments hinge on subtle distinctions. Every sentence, qualification, footnote, citation, and apparatus element in the source text must be meticulously translated without summarisation or omission.
